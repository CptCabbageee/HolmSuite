PlayStation Family Setup

Setting up your PlayStation family

Get your own PlayStation account, and get the kids' profiles using what you pay for. Pick the situation that matches, follow the steps, and ask the helper at the bottom if you get stuck.

1. Make your own account

  1. On a computer or phone, go to PlayStation's Account Management page and choose Create New Account. On a PS5 you can instead go to Switch User → Add User → Create an Account.
  2. Enter your real date of birth and country. You can't change either later, and the kids' accounts must be in the same country as yours.
  3. Use an email address you can get into as your sign-in ID. Open the verification email and follow its link.

It's free, and you don't need to add a card to create the account.

Source: PlayStation: create an adult account

2. Which is true for the kids' profiles?

  1. Sign in to Account Management with your new account.
  2. Go to Family Management → Add Family Member and create a child account for each child. Your account becomes the family manager.
  3. Set each child's spending limit in their parental controls. Children can't add their own cards. They buy from your wallet, up to the limit you set.

Source: PlayStation: set up your family

  2. Answer Yes when it asks if your child already has an account. Enter their sign-in email and date of birth, agree to the terms, then choose Send Family Request.
  3. On the child's account, open Family Management → Received Family Requests, choose the request and select Join Family.

This only works in the UK and Ireland, and both accounts must be from the same country. When the child joins, their saved cards are removed, their games stay, and any subscription they had stops renewing.

Source: PlayStation: send a family request to your child's account

PlayStation says child members can't be removed from a family, and its pages don't describe moving a child to a different family manager. Ask PlayStation Support before trying anything that might close an account. If a family manager closes their own account, it also closes every child account in their family.

If you're already a Parent/Guardian in their dad's family and his PlayStation Plus never reached your console: that's expected. Each account can only share on one PS5, and his is switched on for his own. The fix is your own PlayStation Plus, with Console Sharing and Offline Play turned on for your account on your PS5 (step 3 below). Check afterwards that the kids can play online on their profiles. If they can't, it's because PlayStation ties children's PS Plus sharing to their family manager, so ask PlayStation Support.

  1. Joint option: their dad adds you to his family as an adult and switches on Parent/Guardian for you. You can then set parental controls and see the kids' playtime. You can't spend his wallet, and you can't add or remove members.
  2. Sharing your games: on your PS5, turn on Settings → Users and Accounts → Other → Console Sharing and Offline Play. Anyone who uses that PS5, the kids included, can then play your games. You can only switch it on for one PS5 at a time.
  3. To move them for good: contact PlayStation Support through their Online Assistant and ask whether the children can move to your family.

Sources: set up your family · Console Sharing and Offline Play

3. Share PlayStation Plus with the kids

  1. On the PS5 the kids use, sign in as you and turn on Settings → Users and Accounts → Other → Console Sharing and Offline Play. On a PS4, set it as your Primary PS4 instead.
  2. Online play, games bought with a PS Plus discount, and downloaded monthly PS Plus games are then shared with the other users on that console.

For child accounts, PlayStation says this works when you're their family manager and share the console with them. They only see games their age rating and parental controls allow.

Source: PlayStation: share PlayStation Plus benefits
